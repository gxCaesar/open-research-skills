# Abstract and statistical reporting

## Abstract as a compressed claim map

Write the abstract only after the paper's scientific boundary is stable. Recover five
items from the manuscript and evidence sources:

1. the concrete problem and decision context;
2. the precise unresolved gap;
3. the method or scientific move that addresses it;
4. the main result with its comparison and evaluation scope;
5. the supported conclusion and principal boundary.

Use the venue's current length and formatting rules. Do not impose a remembered word
range. Avoid a list of modules, background survey, fabricated quantitative hook, or a
claim that is stronger than the result section. Every number and comparison in the
abstract must trace to an exact source artifact.

## Report statistics already justified upstream

State the analysis unit, number of independent units, estimand, uncertainty interval or
dispersion, test or model when used, sidedness, multiplicity handling, exclusions, and
missing-data treatment that the locked analysis actually used. Distinguish biological
or subject-level replication from repeated seeds, images, cells, folds, or technical
runs.

Do not introduce a new statistical test during prose polishing, present post-hoc
analysis as prespecified, choose a favorable summary after seeing results, or turn
non-significance into equivalence. If the underlying design is unresolved, report the
gap and route it to the publication pipeline rather than repairing it in wording.

Use consistent notation in the abstract, main text, tables, figures, captions, and
supplement. A corrected statistical sentence is incomplete until every conflicting
instance is reconciled.
