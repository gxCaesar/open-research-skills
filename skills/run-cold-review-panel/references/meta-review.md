# Adjudicating the panel

The meta review is a decision, not a summary and not an average of ratings.

## Adjudicate each finding

For every finding from every lens, decide one of three states.

**Confirmed.** The finding is right about the manuscript. Record what would remove it.

**Unsupported.** The finding rests on something the reviewer misread or on a convention
the venue does not hold. Record why, because the same misreading will recur with a real
reviewer and may be worth pre-empting in the text.

**Underdetermined.** The finding needs evidence the panel did not have. This is a task,
not a verdict.

Agreement across lenses is weak evidence. Lenses that overlap in scope will agree about
whatever is most visible, and a finding raised once by the lens whose job it was is worth
more than the same sentence echoed by four lenses that were reading for something else.

## Prioritise by what a rejection would rest on

Order the confirmed findings by the objection a real reviewer could not be argued out of.
Method correctness and comparison fairness usually rank above presentation, because they
are the ones that cannot be repaired in a rebuttal.

For each, state the repair and its cost: a sentence, a figure, a new control arm, a
re-run. A repair costing a new experiment is a decision for the authors, not for the
panel, and should be presented as such.

## Record the round

Keep per-reviewer reports, the isolation manifest, the executed commands with their
output, and the adjudication in one directory per round. The value of the second round
depends on being able to show what the first one actually said.

## What the panel cannot tell you

It cannot predict acceptance. It has no view of the reviewer pool, the calibration of the
committee, the competing submissions, or the editor. It rehearses objections; it does not
forecast outcomes, and a report that offers a probability of acceptance has exceeded its
evidence.
