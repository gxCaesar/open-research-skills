# The confidentiality boundary

A manuscript under review is unpublished work shared for a single purpose. This
reference is read before the manuscript is opened, because some of its constraints apply
to the act of opening it.

## What the obligation covers

The text, the figures, the supplementary material, the data, the code, the authors'
identities where they are visible, and the fact of the submission itself. Being able to
infer an author's identity does not release you from a double-blind policy.

## Prohibited without explicit permission from the journal

Redistributing the manuscript or any part of it. Quoting it outside the report. Using its
results, methods or ideas in your own work before it is published. Discussing it with
colleagues who are not co-reviewers. Retaining a copy after the review closes, where the
journal asks for deletion.

## Tools and delegation

Pasting a confidential manuscript into an external service transmits it to that service.
Treat that as redistribution unless the journal's policy names the service as permitted.
Policies differ between publishers and are changing; read the current policy for this
journal rather than applying one you remember from another.

The same applies to delegating part of the reading. The obligation travels with the text.
Where a journal permits assistance, it usually requires disclosure; where it does not, a
delegated read is a breach even if nothing leaves your machine.

## Competing interests

Decline when you are working on the same problem, when you have collaborated with the
authors recently, when you have a financial interest in the outcome, or when you cannot
say plainly that you would accept the paper if it were correct.

A competing interest is about the situation, not about your intentions. Declaring it and
letting the editor decide is the correct move, and it costs the editor a day.

## After the review

Follow the journal's instruction on retention. Where none is given, keep nothing beyond
the submitted report. Do not cite an unpublished manuscript you reviewed, and do not
mention having reviewed it.
