# Reading a first submission

Work from claims to evidence, not from the beginning of the paper to the end. Reading in
document order produces a report about presentation, because presentation is what a
linear read encounters first.

## Build the claim table

List every claim the abstract and conclusion make. For each, locate the specific figure,
table or statistic offered in support, and record the gap between what is claimed and
what that evidence establishes.

| Claim | Evidence offered | What it establishes | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|

Claims with no located evidence and evidence supporting no claim are both findings. The
second is common in revised manuscripts and usually indicates a claim that was withdrawn
without removing its apparatus.

## Check the machinery behind the headline number

Ask how the reported number could be wrong even if the method is sound.

Where does the split come from, and can information cross it: repeated measurements of
the same unit, normalisation fitted before splitting, hyperparameters chosen on the test
set, a duplicate that appears on both sides.

What is the independent unit, and does the reported uncertainty use it. Treating
correlated measurements as independent inflates every interval in the paper.

Is the metric defined in the text, and does the definition match the standard one under
the same name.

Were the comparators given a comparable budget, run rather than quoted, and tuned on the
same data.

## Distinguish three verdicts

A result can be wrong, unsupported, or unremarkable, and the three need different
sentences. Wrong means the analysis cannot produce that number. Unsupported means the
number may be right but the evidence shown does not establish it. Unremarkable means it
is correct, established, and changes nothing for the reader.

Only the first two belong at fatal or major severity. The third is a judgement about
significance and should be labelled as one so the editor can weigh it against the
journal's own threshold.

## Write findings, not impressions

A finding names a location, states what is wrong there, and says what would resolve it. A
report of impressions cannot be answered, and an unanswerable report puts the editor in
the position of deciding on your behalf.
