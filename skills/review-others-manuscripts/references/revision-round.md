# Reviewing a revision

A revision round checks three things against each other: what you asked, what the authors
say they did, and what the manuscript now contains. Checking only the first two is the
usual failure, and it is the one the response letter is best at producing.

## Work from the diff, not the letter

Read the marked manuscript before the response letter. The letter is an argument about
the changes; the marked manuscript is the changes. Reading the letter first supplies an
interpretation that the marked text will then appear to confirm.

For each of your original points, record what was requested, what the letter claims, what
the text now says, and whether the change resolves the point.

## Common gaps

A point answered in the letter but not in the manuscript. The reader of the published
paper never sees the letter, so an explanation that lives only there has not been made.

A change that resolves the sentence you objected to while leaving the claim it supported
intact elsewhere. Search for the claim, not for your own wording.

A new analysis added in response that introduces a defect of its own. New material in a
revision receives a first-round read, not a lighter one.

A softened claim that no longer matches the title or the abstract. Revisions routinely
weaken a result in the discussion and leave the headline unchanged.

## Judge the point, not the effort

An extensive response that does not resolve the point leaves the point open. Say so
plainly and without hedging: the editor needs to know whether the objection stands, and a
report that credits effort in place of resolution transfers the decision back to them.

Equally, a one-line change that fully resolves a major point closes it. Length is not the
measure.

## Recommend on the current version

The recommendation applies to the manuscript as it now stands. Points you raised that the
authors declined to address, with reasons you accept, are closed. Points closed by an
argument you do not accept stay open, and the report says why in one sentence each.
