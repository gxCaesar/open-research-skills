# Editorial prescreen

A prescreen decides whether a submission should enter peer review at all. It is a
different judgement from a referee report and should not be written as a short one.

## What a prescreen checks

**Scope.** Does the work address a question the journal publishes on, for the audience it
serves. Reject-without-review on scope is a service to the authors when it is prompt.

**Article type.** Does the submission match a type the journal accepts, with the structure
and limits that type carries. A dataset paper submitted as a research article usually
needs redirection rather than review.

**Policy compliance.** Ethics approval and consent where human or animal subjects are
involved, data and code availability statements, competing interests, authorship and
contribution statements, reporting checklists where the journal mandates them, and prior
publication or preprint status.

**Completeness.** Are the figures legible at submission resolution, the supplementary
files present, the references resolvable, and the methods sufficient for a referee to
judge the work.

## What a prescreen does not do

It does not judge whether the result is correct, novel, or important enough. Those are
referee questions, and answering them at prescreen removes the authors' right to a
substantive review while looking like one.

## The output

A short recommendation with the specific ground: proceed to review, return for a named
missing element, redirect to another article type or journal with the reason, or decline
on scope with one sentence the authors can act on.

Name the policy or the guideline that supports each ground. A prescreen decision without
a citable basis is indistinguishable from a preference, and the authors cannot respond to
it.
