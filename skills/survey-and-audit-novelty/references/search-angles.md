# Searching from several angles

One query framing returns one neighbourhood. A direction that survives a single search is
untested, because the work that would kill it is usually indexed under different words.

## Choosing the angles

Run the search from six to eight independent framings, and record what each returned even
when the answer is nothing. Useful angles include the method family, the application
domain, the evaluation or benchmark name, the data modality, the claimed mechanism, the
adjacent field that solved the same shape of problem, and the negative framing that asks
who tried this and reported it failing.

Angles that return nothing are part of the record. A later reader cannot distinguish "not
searched" from "searched and empty" unless the empty ones are listed.

## Adversarial reading

Assign at least one pass whose stated job is to scoop the idea rather than to confirm it
is new. The confirming pass and the scooping pass find different papers, and only the
second one is evidence.

Judge a candidate collision by reading its method section, not its abstract. Abstracts
compress away exactly the distinctions that decide whether two contributions are the same.

## Recording the survey

For every candidate the search surfaced, record the identifier, the venue and year, what
it actually did, and the disposition: identical, adjacent, irrelevant, or uncertain.

Keep the arithmetic closed. The counts of the dispositions must sum to the number of
candidates examined. A denominator that does not close is hiding a candidate that was
dismissed without a reason.

## What the survey delivers

A survey ends in a decision, not a summary. The deliverable states the lane, the
strongest adjacent work with what it leaves open, the collisions with their layer, the
joint-variable table, and a single recommendation with its reason.
