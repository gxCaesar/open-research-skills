# Rebuttal and discussion revision

## Account for every comment

Create one row per material reviewer or chair comment:

| ID | Exact concern | Type | Factual basis | Existing evidence | Response decision | Manuscript change | Location | Remaining limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Classify the concern as factual correction, missing explanation, missing comparison,
methodological threat, requested experiment, scope disagreement, reproducibility issue,
or presentation issue. Address the decision-critical concerns first, but do not omit
lower-priority comments.

## Choose an honest response decision

- `correct`: acknowledge and fix a factual or presentational defect;
- `clarify`: point to or add explanation supported by existing evidence;
- `new-evidence`: report a permitted, completed analysis with its frozen protocol and
  full result, including negative or inconclusive outcomes;
- `disagree`: explain the exact technical reason and cite the supporting artifact;
- `partial`: state what is resolved and what remains open;
- `limitation`: accept the boundary and narrow the claim.

Cost and authorize any requested experiment before promising it. A planned run remains
future work. Do not call a post-hoc analysis prespecified, tune on a locked test, select
seeds by outcome, or change the metric after seeing results.

## Synchronize the response and manuscript

For every claimed revision, verify the cited section, figure, table, or appendix in the
actual candidate. For every changed manuscript claim, ensure the response explains why
it changed. Preserve the original submission and compare material changes. Respect the
current venue's allowed revision surface, response format, anonymity, and length.

Conference rebuttal or public discussion is not the same artifact as a journal revise
and resubmit package. Do not import a journal response-letter convention or promise a
new central paper unless the conference explicitly permits it.
