# Adversarial journal audit

Audit the exact candidate artifacts by trying to falsify their load-bearing claims and
their package-level consistency.

## Scientific and statistical lenses

Trace each main claim through question, method, data, split, independent unit,
comparison, endpoint, statistical analysis, uncertainty, result, limitation, and exact
artifact. Test leakage, exclusions, missingness, dependence, multiplicity, stopping,
model selection, negative results, causal language, and generalization beyond the
evaluated population or system.

Check whether a simple fair alternative or the strongest relevant comparator closes a
claimed advantage. Repeated seeds, images, cells, fields, or technical measurements do
not become independent biological or subject-level samples by repetition.

## Novelty and citation lens

Compare the exact contribution and evidence against the closest technically relevant
current work. Open primary sources for load-bearing novelty or policy claims. A title,
abstract snippet, or the manuscript's own related-work section does not independently
verify priority. Preserve `UNVERIFIED`, `COULD_NOT_OPEN`, and `NOT_FOUND` states.

## Reporting and policy lens

Apply the current journal and article-type requirements for manuscript structure,
reporting guidelines, ethics, consent, competing interests, authorship, data, code,
materials, image processing, generated imagery, AI use, and disclosures. Separate a
missing statement from missing underlying evidence or access.

## Cross-artifact lens

Compare title page, manuscript, figures, tables, legends, source data, supplement,
checklists, cover letter, response, clean and marked versions, availability statements,
and public artifact references. Inspect rendered pages for clipping, missing fonts,
low-resolution content, wrong numbering, broken links, stale changes, and hidden
identity when anonymity applies.

For every finding, record `observed`, `inferred`, `not_run`, or `failed`; location;
priority; impact; smallest valid action; and settling check. A critical finding must be
grounded in an official rule or directly observed scientific defect.
