# Research idea gates

Apply every gate before including an idea in Section 16.

1. **Traceability:** tie the idea to a specific limitation, failure, assumption,
   contradiction, or unexplained result and cite it.
2. **Hypothesis:** state a proposition that could be false. Reject vague goals such as
   “improve performance” or “use a larger model.”
3. **Delta:** name the single initial change in representation, mechanism, data,
   objective, feedback, evaluation, or deployment constraint.
4. **Matched validation:** first establish that the reference pipeline behaves credibly;
   then compare the proposed delta under the same data, split, representation, budget,
   and metric where possible.
5. **Decision rule:** state the independent unit, expected observation, falsifying result,
   noise floor, go/no-go boundary, and compute or experimental budget when relevant.
6. **Failure modes:** give at least two plausible failures, including attribution error,
   confounding, instability, cost, data scarcity, or invalid assumptions where relevant.
7. **Novelty language:** use `candidate`, `hypothesis`, `possible extension`, and
   `prior-art search required`. Do not use `novel`, `first`, or `unexplored` from the
   paper's framing alone.

For a dedicated prior-art check, keep the search date, sources, exact and adjacent
queries, relevant candidates, access level, and disposition. “No direct hit found”
describes a search outcome; it does not prove that the field is empty.
